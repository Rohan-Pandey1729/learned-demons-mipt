\begin{abstract}
Monitored quantum circuits exhibit a measurement-induced phase transition
(MIPT) between volume-law and area-law entanglement as a function of the
measurement rate $p$. Almost all studies place measurements at random
locations; here we ask what an \emph{agent} in control of measurement
placement can do with the same budget. In brickwork random Clifford circuits
we compare, at exactly matched budget, random placement against hand-designed
and reinforcement-learned placement policies. Three results emerge. First,
placement geometry is more powerful than placement information: a
deterministic contiguous ``lawnmower'' sweep collapses the half-cut entropy
by a factor of several relative to random placement, while equal-coverage
unstructured placement and even a greedy policy with full access to the
quantum state do far worse. Second, the sweep does not merely shift the
critical point --- our data are consistent with it \emph{abolishing} the
volume-law phase: tripartite-mutual-information crossings drift toward
$p \to 0$ with system size, and the steady-state entropy saturates at an
$L$-independent value $\simeq 0.46/p$, as predicted by a ballistic-regrowth
argument in which each site is revisited every $1/p$ layers regardless of
system size; data for $L \le 512$ collapse onto the predicted single-scale
form $S = p^{-1} f(pL)$, with finite-size crossings receding as
$p^\ast \propto 1/L$. Third, we place these
protocols on an exact information ledger: in stabilizer dynamics every
measurement outcome is either deterministic or a perfect coin flip, so the
Shannon entropy of the measurement record --- the demon's minimum memory, and
by Landauer's principle its erasure cost --- is exactly countable. The sweep
dominates the resulting entropy-vs-record-cost frontier at every budget, yet
pays the same $\approx 1$ bit per measurement as random placement: the
demon's advantage lies entirely in \emph{where} it spends equally priced
bits. Reinforcement-learned policies trained from the classical record
underperform the sweep in our experiments \todo{update after PPO runs},
converging instead to a ``fencing'' strategy that quarantines entanglement.
Our results show that the phase diagram of monitored dynamics is a property
of the placement \emph{process}, not merely the measurement rate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Local measurements compete with entangling unitary dynamics: below a critical
measurement rate $p_c$ a monitored many-body system retains volume-law
entanglement, while above it the state collapses to area
law~\cite{Li2018,Skinner2019,Chan2019}. This measurement-induced phase
transition (MIPT) has become a central organizing concept for monitored
quantum matter~\cite{Fisher2023}, with critical properties mapped out in
detail for random Clifford circuits~\cite{Gullans2020,Zabalo2020} and
signatures observed experimentally~\cite{Google2023,Kamakari2025}.

In nearly all of this literature the measured \emph{locations} are chosen
uniformly at random. The rate $p$ is treated as the control parameter, and
placement randomness as an innocuous convention. Existing departures from
uniform randomness are static: quasiperiodic measurement
sublattices~\cite{Shkolnik2023} and quenched rate
disorder~\cite{Zabalo2023} modify the critical point and its universality
class but preserve the transition, while a deterministic spatial
\emph{gradient} of the rate realizes the transition as coexisting phases in
a single chain~\cite{Li2026space}. Separately, \emph{adaptive} circuits
condition unitary feedback on measurement outcomes, producing
absorbing-state physics~\cite{Iadecola2023,ODea2024,Buchhold2022,Piroli2023},
and reinforcement-learning agents have been used to disentangle states with
few projections~\cite{Bao2024}. In all of these, however, \emph{where} to
measure is not the object being designed. \todo{verify framing of
\cite{Bao2024} after full-text read}

Here we take placement itself as the resource. Fixing the measurement budget
--- the expected number of measurements per layer --- we ask how much the
\emph{geometry and information content} of placement decisions matter, and
what they cost. We treat the placing agent as a Maxwell demon: it reads (part
of) the classical measurement record and decides where to look next, and in
stabilizer dynamics its ledger can be kept exactly, because each outcome
carries either zero or exactly one bit of Shannon entropy.

\section{Model and methods}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Monitored Clifford circuits at fixed budget}
We consider a chain of $L$ qubits with periodic boundary conditions evolving
under brickwork layers of independent uniformly random two-qubit Clifford
gates, interleaved with projective single-site $Z$ measurements. In the
standard model each site is measured independently with probability $p$ per
layer. We compare placement \emph{processes} at matched budget $pL$ per
layer: (i) i.i.d.\ random placement; (ii) the \emph{lawnmower}, which
measures the next $m \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(L,p)$ contiguous sites of a
wrapping left-to-right sweep; (iii) equal-coverage controls (round-robin in
shuffled order); (iv) greedy oracle placement by largest local entanglement;
and (v) learned policies (Sec.~\ref{sec:learned}) restricted to classical
record information. Entanglement entropies of stabilizer states are computed
exactly via $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ ranks~\cite{Fattal2004}; simulations use
\texttt{stim}~\cite{Gidney2021}. We use circuit depth $T = 4L$ and locate
transitions with the tripartite mutual information
$\I_3$~\cite{Gullans2020,Zabalo2020} of contiguous quarters.

\subsection{The exact demon ledger}
For stabilizer states, a projective $Z$ measurement outcome is either fully
determined or an unbiased coin flip. The Shannon entropy of the measurement
record in a given trajectory is therefore exactly the number of
nondeterministic outcomes --- a quantity we count during simulation. This is
the minimum memory a demon implementing the policy must write, and by
Landauer's principle, $k_B T \ln 2$ times it bounds the heat cost of erasing
the record~\cite{Landauer1961,Bennett1982}. \todo{connect to purification
framing; possibly relate $\Delta S$ bounds per recorded bit}

\subsection{Baseline}
With random placement, statistically significant pairwise $\I_3$ crossings
among $L = 64$--$512$ (depth $T = 4L$, up to 2000 trajectories per point)
cluster at $p_c = 0.162 \pm 0.008$, with the largest-size pairs at
$p_c \approx 0.159$, consistent with established values for this
model~\cite{Zabalo2020}.

\subsection{The lawnmower}
At matched budget the sweep behaves qualitatively differently
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phaseshift}). Its $\I_3$ crossings do not converge to a
finite rate: the significant pairwise crossings recede with size ---
$(64,128)$: $p^\ast = 0.0085$; $(64,256)$: $p^\ast = 0.0056$ --- and satisfy
$p^\ast \sqrt{L_1 L_2} \approx 0.7$--$0.8$, i.e., $p^\ast \propto 1/L$ at
fixed $p^\ast L$. For the largest pairs the curves become statistically
indistinguishable over a broad window in which $|\I_3| \lesssim 1$,
producing no well-defined crossing at any $p$ in our grid --- consistent
with the crossing having receded below $p = 0.004$. Meanwhile $S(L/2)$
saturates at an $L$-independent value: at $p = 0.004$ the $L = 512$ chain
reaches $S = 106$ bits, close to its predicted ceiling $\simeq 0.46/p = 115$,
and for $p \ge 0.05$ the entropy is size-independent within error bars
across $L = 64$--$512$.

\emph{Ballistic argument.} A sweep at rate $p$ revisits any given site every
$1/p$ layers, independent of $L$. Between visits, entanglement across a cut
regrows at most ballistically, $S \sim v t$, so the steady state saturates
at $S_\mathrm{max} \sim v/p$ for any $L \gg 1/p$: an area law at every fixed
$p > 0$, i.e., $p_c^{\rm sweep} = 0$ in the thermodynamic limit. The
predicted single-scale form $S = p^{-1} f(pL)$, with $f(x) \propto x$ at
small $x$ and $f \to f_\infty$ at large $x$, collapses the data tightly for
$p \le 0.035$ across $L = 64$--$512$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:collapse}), with
$f_\infty \approx 0.46$; larger rates fall below the plateau, as expected
from corrections outside the $p \to 0$ scaling regime. \todo{estimate the
butterfly/entanglement velocity $v$ independently and compare with
$f_\infty$}
